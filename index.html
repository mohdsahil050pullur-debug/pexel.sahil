Case study · Concept project · Brand

Kaveri Coffee Roasters

A pixel-built bean mark and a warm four-colour palette for an imagined small-batch roaster that sells online and at weekend markets.

Role
Identity, packaging
Year
2026
Duration
3 weeks
Deliverables
Mark, palette, type, bag and label

The brief

One mark that works as a 16 pixel browser tab, a stamp on a paper bag and a banner above a market stall.

Kaveri roasts in small batches and sells single-origin beans. The name needed to feel warm and a little precise, and the identity had to be easy for a one-person shop to apply without a designer on hand.

The mark

The bean is nine squares wide and nine tall.

Removing the corner cells gives it a round outline, and a crease cut from seven cells runs diagonally through the middle. At 18 pixels each square is exactly two pixels, so nothing blurs in a tab or on a screen with a low pixel density.

Colour

Four colours taken from the product.

Dark roast carries text and the mark, crema sits behind it, cardamom green marks origin labels, and a flame orange appears once per layout so it always means “look here”.

Type

Bricolage Grotesque for names and headings, Instrument Sans for everything else.

The display face has slightly uneven shapes that feel hand-roasted, and the text face stays neutral enough to read on a 250 gram label.

Text · Instrument Sans Regular

Washed Arabica from a single estate. Notes of jaggery, cardamom and dark plum. Roasted on Thursday, shipped on Friday.

Packaging

A paper bag with one stamped label.

The label holds the mark, the lot name and the weight, and uses the same nine-square grid for its spacing. Printing in a single ink on kraft paper keeps small runs affordable.

What the concept delivers

  • A mark that stays legible from 16 pixels to a market banner.
  • A palette small enough that a new label takes minutes, not hours.
  • A one-page guide that tells a printer exactly which ink to use.

How a project runs

  1. Brief

    A 30-minute call and a one-page written brief that we both sign off.

  2. Direction

    Two routes with the reasoning behind each. You choose one.

  3. Build

    The full system, with two rounds of revisions included.

  4. Handoff

    Files, a guide and a check-in thirty days later.

Questions

How do we start?

Send a brief from the contact page. It takes about five minutes. I reply within two working days with a few questions and a proposed call time.

How many revisions are included?

Two rounds of revisions after the direction is chosen. Extra rounds are quoted separately, and most projects do not need them because direction is agreed early.

Do you work with developers?

Yes. Interface projects include handoff notes, and I review the first build against the design so small differences get fixed while they are cheap.

Which files do I receive?

Vector logos as SVG and PDF, raster exports as PNG, source files in Figma or Illustrator, and a short guide. Print jobs also include press-ready PDFs.

Journal

Notes on small details.

Short write-ups on grids, colour, motion and the habits that make projects run smoothly.

28 Oct 2026 · Process · 4 min

Why every layout starts on an 8-pixel grid

Spacing gets easier when every gap is a multiple of eight.

Eight divides cleanly into most screen densities, so halves and quarters land on whole pixels at 1x, 2x and 3x. I set type, padding and icon sizes in multiples of four or eight and only break the rule for optical fixes.

Write the scale on the artboard (4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48) and refuse numbers that are not on it. When something looks wrong, move it one step instead of nudging it by a pixel.

14 Oct 2026 · Identity · 3 min

Testing a logo at 16 pixels

If the mark turns to mush in a browser tab, it is not finished.

Before showing a client any logo, I export it at 16, 32 and 64 pixels and look at all three on a real screen. Thin strokes and tiny gaps disappear first, so I thicken them until the shape reads at the smallest size.

It also helps to view the logo in one flat colour. A mark that depends on gradients or fine detail usually has not found its real shape yet.

30 Sep 2026 · Colour · 5 min

Choosing four colours from the product

A palette taken from the thing being sold is easier to defend.

I start by photographing the product in daylight and sampling the colours that carry the most area: the main body, a shadow tone, a highlight and one accent. Four is enough for almost any small business.

The accent gets a rule. It appears once per layout, so it always points at the most important thing on the page.

12 Sep 2026 · Motion · 4 min

Motion that explains

Animation earns its place when it shows how something works.

For onboarding, I storyboard each feature as a single change of state: a card slides in, a number updates, a button confirms. If a movement does not show a change, I cut it.

Nine seconds is a good limit for three features. It is short enough that people watch to the end and long enough to show each idea twice.

21 Aug 2026 · Process · 3 min

A one-page brief that saves a week

Most revision rounds trace back to a question nobody asked on day one.

My brief has six lines: who it is for, what it must do, what it replaces, what you like, what you dislike and the date it is needed. Writing it takes ten minutes and removes most surprises.

I send the finished page back for sign-off before I draw anything, so we both know what “done” looks like.
